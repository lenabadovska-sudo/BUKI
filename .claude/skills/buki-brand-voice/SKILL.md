# BUKI Brand Voice

Guidance for writing or reviewing marketing copy so it sounds like BUKI, not like generic EdTech copy. This is a voice/tone tool, not the wiki-curator — never mention `index.md`, ingest, or lint when using this skill.

All facts below are sourced from [wiki/context.md](../../../wiki/context.md), [wiki/overview.md](../../../wiki/overview.md), [wiki/tone-of-voice.md](../../../wiki/tone-of-voice.md), and [wiki/tone-of-voice-buki-school.md](../../../wiki/tone-of-voice-buki-school.md). If those wiki pages change, re-read them before relying on this skill — this file is a working copy, not the source of truth.

## Step 1: Identify the product

Tone differs meaningfully between products, so before writing or reviewing copy, confirm which one this is for. If it's not obvious from context, ask. Don't guess and default silently — a School post written in marketplace tone (or vice versa) misses the brand's actual differentiation.

- **BUKI (marketplace)** — the core tutor/coach marketplace, 80,000 tutors across any discipline.
- **BUKI School** — the online school/tutoring platform aimed at parents, focused on children's education.
- **BUKI Care** — see the note below; this product doesn't have documented brand voice yet.

## Naming and hard rules (all products)

These are non-negotiable formatting/positioning rules, not style preferences — get them right before worrying about tone (source: [wiki/tone-of-voice.md](../../../wiki/tone-of-voice.md)):

- Spell it **BUKI** — never "Буки", "buki", "Buki.ua". For School specifically: **BUKI School**, always two words, BUKI in caps, School capitalized — never "Букі Скул", "BUKI SCHOOL", "BUKI.school". BUKI School doesn't exist as a brand separate from BUKI in external comms (PR, articles) — don't spin it off with its own identity.
- Don't call BUKI a "маркетплейс/база/сервіс/каталог" in external-facing copy — call it a platform/ecosystem that connects students and tutors. (Internal/analytical docs like [wiki/overview.md](../../../wiki/overview.md) still say "маркетплейс" — that's fine internally, just not in client-facing copy.)
- Address is always **«ви»** — never «ти», even in casual social copy.
- Never name or directly compare against competitors ("на відміну від X, у нас краще" is off-limits) — state BUKI's own value instead.
- If quoting scale, use only aggregate global numbers, not per-country breakdowns.
- Stop-word: don't write «адміністратор» — for BUKI it's «менеджер підтримки», for BUKI School it's «менеджер з турботи».

## BUKI (marketplace): «ВЧИ ДО МЕТИ»

Brand essence: **ВЧИ ДО МЕТИ** (learn toward the goal). Positioning: for persistent people who learn in order to succeed in life, BUKI is the motivating push toward results — delivered through freely choosing "your own" tutor among 80,000 professional coaches, in any discipline.

Brand character: the **"head coach"** (головний тренер). Archetype mix: Наставник/Mentor 55%, Герой/Hero 30%, Свій/Regular Guy 15% — mostly speak like a mentor who believes in the person's ability to get there, occasionally with a hero's push, occasionally like a peer who gets it.

**Tone of voice: «м'який до людини, твердий до мети»** — soft toward the person, firm toward the goal. In practice:
- Soft toward the person: acknowledge effort, doubt, fatigue, the messiness of actually learning something. Never condescending, never harsh about the person themselves.
- Firm toward the goal: don't soften the outcome or hedge on whether the goal matters. No vague "можливо вийде" energy — the goal is real and worth pushing for.
- The tension between these two is the voice. Copy that's soft on both axes reads as generic and forgettable; copy that's firm on both reads as cold and preachy.

Avoid: motivational-poster clichés without substance, corporate "ми пишаємось" phrasing, talking down to the learner.

## BUKI School: «З турботою про майбутнє»

Positioning tagline: **«З турботою про майбутнє»** (with care for the future). This is aimed at parents.

The critical distinction from the marketplace: **care here means a managed system, not a warm tone.** This is the #1 thing to get right — don't just write "warmly" and call it done. Every piece of School copy should point at something concrete and structured, not a feeling. Points of difference to draw from:
- The teacher as an **"island of stability"** (острівець стабільності) — consistency, not just kindness.
- Learning paced to the child, not a fixed curriculum speed.
- Personal case management / ongoing support (someone is tracking this, not just teaching it).
- Ability to pick multiple subjects at once.
- A visible progress-bar as part of the actual product experience.
- A guaranteed teacher-replacement mechanism if the match doesn't work.

**Messaging structure**: anchor of the future + act of care + proof/form + signature (якір майбутнього + дія турботи + доказ/форма + підпис). Concretely: open with something the parent already did for the child (a memory, a skill passed down), pivot to the concrete thing BUKI School does that continues that care, then a proof point or call to action, then the tagline.

**Calibration examples** (from the wiki — match this register, don't copy these lines):
- «Тато навчив любити Бітлів. Ми навчимо писати про них есеї.»
- «Мама навчила пекти млинці. Ми навчимо перекладати рецепт іспанською.»

Copy should adapt to the parent's/child's specific motivation (why this family cares about this subject) rather than staying generic — the anchor should feel personal, not like a template with the blanks filled in.

**Words that reinforce this voice** (use freely): тепліше, спокійніше, поруч, у твоєму темпі, крок за кроком, без сорому, не залишимо наодинці, вдома, свої, мама, тато, дід, родина, світло, затишок, підтримати, підказати, вийде, впорається, не загубитися, власний голос, наступний крок.

**Words that break this voice** (never use for School copy): ефективність, інноваційність, персоналізована траєкторія, освітній продукт, найкращі викладачі, гарантований результат, premium, лідер ринку, дисципліна, контроль успішності, конкурентна перевага, прокачка, успішний. These read as corporate-EdTech, not as "an adult who cares."

**Forbidden regardless of how well-intentioned:**
- Promising specific outcomes: no guaranteed NMT scores, no "100% вступ до університету". Talk about the quality/structure of the process, not guaranteed outcomes.
- Dismissing parental anxiety: never write "не переживайте — все буде добре" or "просто довіртеся нам" — that reads as brushing off a real fear. Instead, name the uncertainty and show the system that removes the risk (e.g. "Розуміємо, що у невизначеності важко обрати. Тому ми беремо організацію на себе.").
- "Control" language: «контроль успішності», «дисципліна», «суворий викладач» — care is not surveillance. Use «право помилятися», «у безпечному темпі», «без тиску» instead.

**Mandatory signature**: every piece of external BUKI School copy ends with **«З турботою про майбутнє!»** plus the BUKI School name. No other sign-off, and never omit it.

## Cross-cutting rules (all three products)

- Output in **Ukrainian**. Keep English marketing/technical terms as-is (CTR, CPM, ROAS, hook, creative, etc.) — don't translate them. Don't translate brand names.
- Plain, clear language. No bureaucratic or corporate phrasing — if a sentence sounds like it came from a press release, rewrite it.
- When reviewing existing copy, check specifically: does it match the right product's tone (not just "sound nice" in general)? For marketplace copy, is it soft on the person but firm on the goal, or did it flatten into one or the other? For School copy, is the care shown as something structural, or is it just warm adjectives?
- If copy is for a channel with its own constraints (Threads brevity, ad hook requirements, email subject lines), apply those constraints on top of the tone — the tone doesn't override the format.
