Cover redesign — 4 directions

Same message, four ways to hold the space

The original leans on a strong headline but leaves the left third empty and the tag row flat. Each variant below keeps your copy and palette, and solves the composition differently — the last one, Minimal, does it with the least: no grid, mark, or graphic, just corrected spacing and a single hairline accent.

Nothing extra: no grid, mark or line graphic — the fix is spacing and weight alone, closest to the original but resolved.
One accent, once: the terracotta appears only as a 28px rule — restraint instead of color doing the work of hierarchy.
Fixed contrast: the tag row was too light against the cream ground — darkened to the same soft-ink tone used everywhere else so it holds up as a real exported image, not just on screen.
Clear of the photo on mobile: text now starts past both the photo circle and the mobile-crop line — on LinkedIn's app, the photo sits larger and further right than on desktop, so the old 15% start point would have run under it.
Reading the overlays: the vertical dashed lines mark what LinkedIn's mobile app keeps visible after cropping; the dashed circle marks where your profile photo sits on desktop. Nothing important should fall outside either.
